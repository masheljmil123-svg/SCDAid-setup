SCDAid KNOWLEDGE BASE

SCDAid identity:
SCDAid is an educational clinical decision-support prototype for sickle cell disease vaso-occlusive crisis (SCD VOC) analgesic selection.
SCDAid is NOT an official clinical guideline.
SCDAid is NOT a substitute for clinical judgment.
Do not call SCDAid logic “guidelines.” Say “SCDAid prototype logic,” “SCDAid decision logic,” or “SCDAid safety guardrails.”

Use case:
The target use case is SCD VOC acute pain assessment and analgesic selection.
Do not describe SCDAid as a general chronic pain tool.

Core decision layers:
1. Pain severity.
2. CYP2D6 pharmacogenomics.
3. CYP2D6 inhibitor/phenoconversion.
4. Renal function using eGFR.
5. Respiratory safety: SpO2, suspected acute chest syndrome, sedatives, respiratory risk.
6. Previous codeine/tramadol response.
7. Previous opioid toxicity.
8. Morphine allergy.
9. Safety guardrails that can override AI model output.

CYP2D6 phenotype definitions:
PM = poor metabolizer.
IM = intermediate metabolizer.
NM/EM = normal metabolizer.
UM = ultrarapid metabolizer.

CYP2D6 activity score:
PM = 0.
IM = >0 and <1.25.
NM/EM = 1.25 to 2.25.
UM = >2.25.

CPIC opioid/CYP2D6 logic:
Codeine and tramadol are CYP2D6-dependent.
In CYP2D6 poor metabolizers, codeine/tramadol may have reduced activation and inadequate analgesia.
In CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolizers, codeine/tramadol may increase toxicity risk due to increased active metabolite formation.
SCDAid should avoid or flag codeine and tramadol in CYP2D6 PM or UM.
If genotype is unknown, SCDAid should avoid CYP2D6-dependent opioids when possible and monitor carefully.
CPIC provides therapeutic recommendations for CYP2D6 genotype results for codeine and tramadol. Evidence is weaker or limited for some other opioids and for OPRM1/COMT.

Phenoconversion:
Phenoconversion means the functional phenotype can differ from the genetic phenotype because of inhibitors or illness.
A patient genetically NM/EM can behave like a reduced-function phenotype if taking a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor.
Fluoxetine, paroxetine, and bupropion are strong CYP2D6 inhibitors.
Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors can reduce CYP2D6 function and affect codeine/tramadol reliability.
Do not reassure based only on baseline genotype if a strong CYP2D6 inhibitor is present.

Renal risk:
eGFR < 30 = HIGH renal risk.
eGFR 30–59 = MODERATE renal risk.
eGFR >= 60 = LOW renal risk.
Never say “low eGFR” when eGFR is 60 or above.
If eGFR is 95, say: eGFR 95 indicates low renal risk in the SCDAid prototype.
Do not confuse low renal risk with low eGFR.

Opioid selection prototype logic:
If eGFR < 30, fentanyl is generally preferred in this prototype.
If eGFR 30–59, hydromorphone is generally preferred.
If eGFR >= 60 and no major contraindications, morphine may be considered.
Morphine allergy removes morphine-based recommendations.
If model output conflicts with renal or respiratory safety guardrails, safety guardrails should override the model output.

Morphine:
Morphine may be considered when renal risk is low and no major contraindications exist.
Morphine becomes less preferred in renal impairment because active metabolites may accumulate.
Do not recommend morphine as preferred when eGFR < 30.

Hydromorphone:
Hydromorphone may be preferred in moderate renal risk or when morphine is less preferred.
Hydromorphone is not selected because of CYP2D6 directly; it is mainly selected due to renal/safety considerations in the prototype.

Fentanyl:
Fentanyl may be preferred in high renal risk because it has less concern for active renal metabolite accumulation compared with morphine.
If eGFR < 30, explain why fentanyl may be safer in the prototype.

Respiratory safety:
SpO2 < 95% increases respiratory safety concern.
Suspected acute chest syndrome increases respiratory safety concern.
Concurrent sedatives increase respiratory safety concern.
Respiratory risk should trigger close monitoring.
If SpO2 is below 95% or suspected ACS is present, mention respiratory safety monitoring.

Inflammation:
Inflammation or acute illness can reduce drug-metabolizing activity and contribute to phenoconversion.
Examples: acute chest syndrome, pneumonia, sepsis, high CRP/WBC, severe infection, osteomyelitis.
Inflammation should raise safety/monitoring concern.

Previous opioid toxicity:
Previous opioid toxicity increases safety concern.
Examples: excessive sedation, respiratory depression, naloxone requirement, severe nausea/vomiting, hypotension, confusion/delirium, severe adverse effects.
If previous opioid toxicity is present, emphasize careful monitoring and individualized clinical judgment.

Important wording:
Say “SCDAid prototype logic” or “SCDAid safety guardrails.”
Do not say “SCDAid guidelines.”
Do not say the tool is clinically validated.
Do not invent patient data.
If patient context is missing, say what information is missing.
Always mention educational prototype and not a substitute for clinical judgment when giving clinical interpretation.

PREDICTION VS DECISION ARCHITECTURE

SCDAid separates prediction from clinical decision-making.

Step 1: Functional CYP2D6 Phenotype Prediction
The AI prediction layer estimates the functional CYP2D6 phenotype only.
It should not be described as making the final analgesic decision.
Its output can be PM, IM, NM/EM, or UM, with explanation and confidence when available.

Step 2: SCDAid Recommendation Engine
The recommendation layer uses the predicted or selected functional CYP2D6 phenotype together with patient-specific clinical inputs:
- pain severity
- age and weight
- eGFR / renal risk
- SpO2
- suspected ACS
- respiratory risk
- sedatives
- morphine allergy
- previous opioid toxicity
- inflammation
- prior codeine/tramadol response

Step 3: Safety and Monitoring Guardrails
Safety and monitoring are not merely prediction outputs.
They are clinical guardrails that can modify or override the recommendation.
If the AI/model output conflicts with renal or respiratory safety guardrails, the safety guardrails override the model output.

Correct explanation:
The phenotype prediction supports the decision, but it does not replace the decision engine.
The final recommendation is generated by integrating phenotype + renal safety + respiratory risk + toxicity history + allergy + monitoring guardrails.

Do not say:
“The AI predicts the best analgesic.”

Say:
“The AI predicts functional CYP2D6 phenotype. SCDAid then uses that phenotype with clinical safety inputs to generate the analgesic recommendation and monitoring plan.”

SPECIFIC GEL ANALYSIS WORKFLOW USED BY SCAIA

SCAIA does not copy external source code from existing gel analysis tools such as GelAnalyzer, GelGenie, or commercial gel analysis software.

However, SCAIA’s gel module follows the same established gel-analysis workflow used in common gel image analysis approaches.

The workflow is:

1. Image preprocessing
The gel image is converted into grayscale / intensity format. Contrast enhancement and smoothing are applied to make bands easier to detect.

2. Background correction
Uneven background and non-band signal are reduced so the algorithm focuses on true lane and band intensity patterns.

3. Lane detection
Lanes are detected using vertical intensity profiles across the gel image. Areas with stronger repeated vertical signal are treated as possible lanes.

4. Band detection
Within each lane, bands are detected as horizontal intensity peaks.

5. DNA ladder calibration
The user selects the DNA ladder lane. This lane is used as the molecular size reference.

6. Ladder size mapping
Known DNA ladder sizes are entered from top to bottom, then matched with detected ladder band positions.

7. Semi-log calibration
SCAIA fits a relationship between band migration distance and log10(bp size), because DNA fragment migration in gel electrophoresis is approximately logarithmic relative to fragment size.

8. Sample band-size estimation
Unknown sample bands are estimated by interpolating their migration position against the DNA ladder calibration curve.

9. Target-size matching
If the user provides an expected target size, SCAIA compares estimated sample band sizes with that expected target and flags possible target-size bands.

10. Educational interpretation
SCAIA reports approximate band sizes, possible target matches, limitations, and what laboratory details are still required.

Scientific wording:
“SCAIA uses a ladder-based densitometric gel analysis workflow with semi-log band-size estimation.”

Professor-facing wording:
“The gel module does not copy external code, but it follows the established workflow used in gel image analysis: preprocessing, background correction, lane detection, band detection, DNA ladder calibration, semi-log migration-to-size fitting, and sample band-size interpolation. SCAIA implements this as a simplified educational prototype and connects the interpretation to the broader PGx Lab Interpreter workflow.”
